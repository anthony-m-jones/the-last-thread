# THE LAST THREAD
### A small metroidvania — narrative, environments & dialogue

*(All names are placeholders — swap freely. The orange cat is intentionally **unnamed** for most of the game; that's a payoff, explained at the end.)*

---

## The emotional spine

Everything in the game is one idea wearing three costumes: **the things we're afraid of are usually the things that love us.**

- The cat is afraid to let the girl, **Ella**, pet him — even though he loves her.
- The cat is afraid of the **spiders**, and especially their hidden leader — even though they're gentle.
- Ella, we slowly learn, is the one human who is gentle to spiders: she carries them outside on a leaf instead of crushing them. *That's why the spiders love her.* It's also the mirror the whole game turns on — the feared thing (spider, skittish cat) turns out to be safe, if you're brave enough to stay still.

The three abilities map to three escalating acts of courage:

| Room | Ability | Inner lesson | Mood |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Jump** | You were always able. Fear made you forget you're *allowed.* | Cozy, melancholy, beginning |
| 2 | **Wall-jump** | The walls that stop you can also lift you. Keep climbing. | Cold, striving, persistence |
| 3 | **Dash** | Don't wait. Close the distance while you still can. | Warm, magical, arrival |

The "thing the spiders hid" stays a secret until Room 3. The reveal: it's a **silk tapestry** the spiders spent a long time weaving — a portrait of Ella with the orange cat asleep in her lap. **A picture of a moment that hasn't happened yet.** And it's *unfinished* on purpose: only the cat can complete it, by actually doing the thing it shows.

---

## The cast

- **The cat** — orange, shy, terse. Speaks rarely and plainly (render his lines as meows + subtitle, or thought-text). Has no name: he's never let anyone get close enough to give him one. Ella *almost* names him — she always gets out "Tob—" before he bolts.
- **The Weaver** — the leader spider. Always *partially* hidden: a single long leg in a shaft of light, eight points of glinting eyes in the dark, a voice that comes from above and behind you. Speaks grand, old-fashioned, and dry — but every word he says is kind, and you only realize that in hindsight. Calls the cat **"little ember."**
- **Pip** — a kitten. Gives **Jump.** Pure joy, no fear yet.
- **Patch** — a one-eyed alley cat. Gives **Wall-jump.** Scarred, dry-humored, kind underneath.
- **Marigold** — an old, dignified house cat. Gives **Dash.** Carries the one bittersweet lesson in the whole game.

# ROOM 1 — THE GARDEN AT DUSK
*Intro. Establishes cat, Ella, spiders. Ability: **Jump.** Puzzle: **hedge maze.***

### Environment

An overgrown autumn garden in the last twenty minutes of daylight. This is the warm, sad "home base" of the whole game.

- **Look:** Tall dry grass, a leaning wooden fence, a cracked stone birdbath, fat pumpkins gone soft, a forgotten ball of yarn snagged on a thorn. A trellis and a tool shed give you things to climb once you have Jump.
- **Color & light:** Burnt orange, dusty rose, deepening violet. Long shadows. Fireflies start to wake as the scene darkens — they double as soft light-guides toward the next platform.
- **The detail that sells it:** Far in the background, *unreachable,* a single window in a house glows warm gold. That window is **Ella.** It's always on screen, always too far. The whole first room is the cat being close to her and unable to reach.
- **Sound:** Crickets, a wind chime from the house, the rustle of grass.
- **Traversal & puzzle:** You start at ground level, boxed in. Pip gives you Jump near the start. You jump up crates → trellis → shed roof → into the **hedge maze** at the top of the garden. The maze is a "find your way when you're lost" puzzle — fitting, since the cat is lost. Fireflies cluster at correct turns. Completing it spins open a gate woven shut with silk: the door to Room 2.

### Opening cutscene — *The Cat & The Weaver*

> The cat creeps in chasing a single drifting strand of silk. It leads to the dark heart of the hedge. Something enormous shifts in there. Only a leg emerges into the light.

**WEAVER:** *...So. The little ember comes. Closer. No — not too close. I am easier to bear in pieces.*

**CAT:** *(low)* You're the one the others whisper about.

**WEAVER:** *I am the one the others whisper *to.* There is a difference, though I'll not waste dusk explaining it.* *(a pause; the eyes turn toward the glowing window)* *You watch that window. Every evening. From exactly this far.*

**CAT:** *(says nothing)*

**WEAVER:** *We watch it too. The girl there is kind to small frightened things — kinder than the world taught her to be. We have made her something. A thing of thread and patience. We hid it deep, where it would be safe.* *(the leg withdraws into shadow)* *Bring it back to her. You needn't know yet what it is. You need only know you're the right one to carry it.*

**CAT:** Why me?

**WEAVER:** *...You'll answer that yourself, by the end. Now. You've forgotten how to leave the ground. Someone here will remind you.*

### Ability cat — *Pip (Jump)*

> A kitten is bouncing off a mushroom for no reason at all, delighted every single time.

**PIP:** Hi hi hi! Did you SEE that?! I did it again! Watch — *(boing)* — AGAIN!

**CAT:** I don't jump.

**PIP:** What? Everybody jumps. You're *built* for it. Look at your legs! Those are jumping legs!

**CAT:** It's a long way down.

**PIP:** Sure, but the good stuff's *up.* *(stops bouncing, suddenly earnest in the way only kittens are)* You're not scared of falling. You're scared of *being allowed.* It's okay. You're allowed. *(grins)* Watch me, then copy me. The first one's the only hard one.

> **— Jump unlocked. —**

**PIP:** *(as you leave)* Hey! When you find what you're looking for — jump toward it, okay? Don't just look!

### Closing beat (optional, on maze completion)

> The silk gate unspools. The Weaver's voice drifts from nowhere and everywhere.

**WEAVER:** *One leap, and already higher than you've ever stood. Remember that, ember. The ground lied to you about how far you could go.*

# ROOM 2 — THE GLASS HOUSE IN THE RAIN
*Ability: **Wall-jump.** Puzzle: a vertical climb. Theme: setbacks, persistence.*

### Environment

An abandoned greenhouse at night, rain drumming on the roof. Everything here is **vertical** — the room is taller than it is wide, and you feel the height as a challenge.

- **Look:** A glass-and-iron skeleton, half its panes cracked or gone. Overgrown vines climb the walls. Tiered shelves at staggered heights, terracotta pots, a tall rusted central column, a dead chandelier of dried flowers hanging high above.
- **Color & light:** Cold blues, wet greens, a sickly moon through wet glass. Lightning occasionally flashes — and in those flashes you glimpse, for one frame, the silhouette of the Weaver suspended high in the rafters, far bigger than feels reasonable. *(Use the flash sparingly. Two or three times total. It should make players gasp, not get used to it.)*
- **The detail that sells it:** The rain on the glass casts running shadows down the walls — it looks like the whole room is *weeping.* This is the discouragement room. The cat has been knocked down, doubts the quest, doubts himself.
- **Sound:** Heavy rain, dripping, the groan of old iron, distant thunder.
- **Traversal & puzzle:** Patch gives you Wall-jump. You scale the broken glass walls and the central column, bouncing between vines and panes, climbing toward the high chandelier. The puzzle: a route that *looks* like a dead end at every level — until you realize the walls themselves are the path. Reaching the top unwinds another silk-sealed door.

### Opening cutscene — *The Cat & The Weaver*

> The cat shelters under a leaf from the rain, soaked, discouraged. The Weaver's eyes appear in the dark above, upside-down.

**WEAVER:** *You're wet, and you're doubting. Good. Doubt means you understand what's being asked.*

**CAT:** I can't reach it. Whatever it is. It's too high. There's a wall.

**WEAVER:** *There is *always* a wall. I have spent my whole long life among walls.* *(a strand of silk lowers, catches the moonlight)* *Here is the secret no spider tells: a wall is only a barrier if you push *against* it. Push *off* it, and it throws you higher than open air ever could.* *(the eyes soften — or maybe that's the rain)* *The girl you love is a wall, too, ember. Skittish thing — you keep hurling yourself at her and bouncing off and calling it failure. It isn't. It's the climb.*

**CAT:** ...How do you know about her.

**WEAVER:** *I told you. We watch the window.* *(a low, dry sound that might be a laugh)* *She carries my children outside on a leaf, you know. When the others would crush them. She has never once been afraid of the right things.* *(withdraws)* *Now climb. The one who taught me about walls is here. He learned it the hard way, as the best ones do.*

### Ability cat — *Patch (Wall-jump)*

> A battered alley cat sits on a high ledge, watching the rain through his one good eye. He doesn't turn around.

**PATCH:** You're the one the spider's running errands for. Heard about you.

**CAT:** Everyone's heard about me but me, apparently.

**PATCH:** *(snorts)* Funny. Most cats your size don't have a sense of humor. Too busy being scared. *(finally looks at you — the scars, the missing eye)* You want to get up there? You gotta stop seeing walls as the end of the road. I got every one of these falling *off* things. And then one day I figured out the wall was the only friend I had — push off it, you go up. Hit it, kick away, hit the next one. You don't have to make it all the way in one go. You just can't stop bouncing.

**CAT:** And if I fall?

**PATCH:** Then you fall, and you climb again, and nobody up there's keeping score but you. *(half a grin)* Quit keeping score. Climb.

> **— Wall-jump unlocked. —**

**PATCH:** *(as you go)* Hey, orange. Whoever's up that high that you're climbing for — she's worth the falling. Has to be, or you wouldn't be this scared. That's how you know.

### Closing beat (optional, at the top)

> Reaching the chandelier, the cat is exhausted but high above the rain now, the storm below him.

**WEAVER:** *You see? From down there it was a prison of glass. From up here it is a lantern in the dark. Nothing changed but how high you were willing to climb. One room left, ember. Come and learn what you've been carrying toward.*

# ROOM 3 — THE HEART OF THE OAK
*Ability: **Dash.** Final puzzle: **trivia.** Theme: commitment, vulnerability, the secret revealed.*

### Environment

Inside the hollow of an ancient oak — the spiders' home, and the most beautiful place in the game. The "spooky" reveal that turns sweet: the feared spiders have built a cathedral.

- **Look:** A vast vertical chamber inside living wood. Thousands of silk strands stretch wall to wall, every one beaded with dew that catches the light like a galaxy. Woven silk lanterns hang at all heights. Tiny spiders go about their work, and they are *gentle,* unhurried, warm. Walkways of taut silk thread.
- **Color & light:** Deep warm gold and amber returns — but magical now, glittering. Glowworms. After the cold rain of Room 2, walking in here should feel like coming home.
- **The detail that sells it:** At the very top, draped and half-finished, is the **silk tapestry.** You can't see what it depicts until the reveal. The little spiders keep glancing at the cat and then at each other, hopeful — *they've been waiting for him.*
- **Sound:** A soft resonant hum, like wind over harp strings (the silk vibrating). The first time the music turns *gentle* in the whole game.
- **Traversal & puzzle:** Marigold gives you Dash. You dash across gaps in the silk strands, through closing apertures, up to the tapestry at the top — where the Weaver waits, and the final test (trivia) gates the gift.

### Opening cutscene — *The Cat & The Weaver*

> The cat steps in and stops dead at the beauty of it. For the first time the Weaver is *nearly* visible — a great shape settled among the lanterns, but lit warm, no longer menacing.

**WEAVER:** *Welcome, little ember. To the place we hid the thing we made.* *(the little spiders part to show the way up)* *You were afraid of us. From the first dusk. I let you be — fear is a fine teacher, in small doses.* *But look around you now. Tell me: what is so frightening here?*

**CAT:** *(quietly)* ...Nothing. It's beautiful.

**WEAVER:** *Yes. We are the thing in the dark that everyone runs from. And we spend our lives making beauty no one stays long enough to see.* *(a beat — pointed)* *Does that remind you of anyone, ember? Some small orange creature who is gentle and good and runs the moment a kind hand reaches for him?*

**CAT:** *(no answer — which is an answer)*

**WEAVER:** *The girl is not afraid of us. She is the one soul who kneels down to the frightening thing and is kind to it anyway. That is the rarest courage there is.* *(softening fully)* *And it is the courage I am asking of *you* now. Not toward me. Toward her. But first — one last thread to teach you. Then I will show you what you've carried all this way to give.*

### Ability cat — *Marigold (Dash)*

> An old, elegant cat sits perfectly still on a high silk platform, watching the dew. She speaks slowly, kindly, and it lands like a quiet gut-punch.

**MARIGOLD:** Come, little one. Sit a moment with me. There's time. *(beat)* ...That's the lie, you know. *"There's time."* I told it to myself for sixteen years.

**CAT:** What do you mean?

**MARIGOLD:** I had a person. A good one. I loved her from the windowsill across the room. Every day I told myself — *tomorrow* I'll cross. Tomorrow I'll curl up in her lap. I had a thousand tomorrows and I spent every one of them being almost-brave. *(quietly)* And then one day there were no more tomorrows, and I had loved her perfectly, from exactly far enough away to never be loved back.

**CAT:** *(very quiet)* ...Oh.

**MARIGOLD:** So I'll give you the only thing I have worth giving. *Dash.* The art of crossing the room before you can talk yourself out of it. Not a slow brave walk — there's too much time in a walk to turn around. A *dash.* You see the lap. You go. You arrive before the fear catches up. *(she rises, suddenly spry)* Now. Cross to me. Don't think. Go.

> **— Dash unlocked. —**

**MARIGOLD:** *(gently, as you leave)* You're going to her, aren't you. *(a soft, glad sound)* Then don't you dare be me. Dash, little ember.

### THE FINAL PUZZLE — *Trivia: "Do you truly know her?"*

> The cat dashes up to the tapestry. The Weaver stands beside it, still draped so the image is hidden.

**WEAVER:** *Here it is. The thing of thread and patience. But I will not let you carry it to her on love alone. Plenty of creatures *want* someone. Few have ever truly *seen* them.* *(the eyes fix on the cat)* *Love is only attention that lasted. So tell me, ember — these long dusks at the window. Were you watching her? Or only watching the warm light she happened to stand in?*

> **Trivia begins.** *(This does the clever double job of characterizing Ella — who never appears on screen — through the cat's own knowledge of her. Every right answer is a brick of who she is. Suggested questions; tune to your taste. Wrong answers don't fail you — the Weaver gently corrects and lets you try again, because the point is learning her, not being punished.)*

1. **When Ella finds a spider in the house, what does she do?**
   → *Carries it outside on a leaf.* (Correct: *"Yes. Now you understand why we love her."*)
2. **What does she hum when she thinks no one is listening?**
   → *No real tune — just whatever's in her head.* (Correct: *"Off-key. Constantly. It's perfect."*)
3. **Why does she leave the garden window open even in the cold?**
   → *For the orange cat, in case he ever wants to come in.* (Correct: *"...She has been waiting for you longer than you know, ember."*)
4. **What is the name she almost calls you, the one you always run from?**
   → ***Tobers.*** (Correct — and the Weaver goes quiet a moment.)

> On the final answer:

**WEAVER:** *Tobers.* *(a long pause)* *You knew it all along. You ran every time, not because you didn't want it — but because once she finished saying it, you'd be hers, and there would be no more running. You've been afraid of being *home.** *(he steps aside)* *Then let me show you what we wove for her. And for you.*

### THE REVEAL

> The silk falls away. The tapestry is a portrait, woven in shining thread: **Ella, sitting in her chair by the open window — with the orange cat asleep in her lap.** A perfect, impossible image. But the cat's shape in the picture is woven only in faint, unfinished outline — a ghost, a hope.

**CAT:** *(staring)* That's... that hasn't happened. I've never— I always run before—

**WEAVER:** *I know. That is why your part is only outline.* *(gently)* *We can weave everything but the one thread that matters. We cannot make you stay in her lap. Only you can spin that. The gift was never finished, ember — because the gift is **you, choosing to be brave once, all the way.** Take it to her. And this time — don't run.*

# THE ENDING
*(Ella stays off-screen, as you wanted — heard, never shown. The whole emotional weight lands on sound and on what we *don't* see.)*

> Final sequence — playable or cutscene. The cat carries a strand of the silk out into the dawn garden of Room 1. The unreachable golden window is now a warm **open door,** finally at ground level. We see the cat from behind, small, facing the light. Inside, a girl's shape we never quite see.

**ELLA (off-screen):** Oh... *(soft)* Hey. It's you. You came back. You always come back, and then you— *(a held breath)*

> The cat does not run. *(If playable: the last input is a single Dash, toward the door. One button. Everything the game taught, in one press.)* The cat steps inside.

**ELLA (off-screen, delighted, gentle):** ...Tobers.

> This time he doesn't bolt. The screen holds on the open door, the warm light, the dawn. We hear a chair creak, a girl settle, a quiet *"there you are."* And then — for the first time in the whole game — **purring.**

> The camera drifts up to the rafters of the porch, where a single long leg rests in the shadow, and eight small points of light watch, content. The little spiders gather around the Weaver.

**LITTLE SPIDER:** Did he do it, Grandfather? Did he stay?

**WEAVER:** *(softly, the warmest he's ever sounded)* *He stayed. The last thread is spun.* *(a beat)* *Come. We have a great deal of weeping in the rain ahead of us — there are always more frightened little embers in the world who've forgotten they're allowed.*

> **THE END.**
> *(The save screen / title card now shows the tapestry — completed. The cat's outline is filled in solid gold at last, asleep in Ella's lap. The image you spent the whole game earning.)*

## A few production notes to make it strike home

- **Keep the Weaver hidden almost to the end, and only ever reveal him as *gentle,* never as a monster.** The lightning-flash silhouette in Room 2 is your one scare. Everything after pays off the subversion. If you show too much, you lose the "feared thing is kind" theme that carries the whole story.
- **The golden window is your throughline prop.** Unreachable (R1) → glimpsed from above (R2 as a far light) → an open door at ground level (ending). The player should feel the distance close without you ever saying it.
- **Withhold the cat's name.** "Tobers" only appears in the Room 3 trivia and the final line. The player feels the weight of him never having been named because no one ever got close enough.
- **Let wrong trivia answers be kind, not punishing.** The Weaver correcting you teaches Ella's character; failure states would break the tone.
- **Music:** keep it sparse and a little lonely in Rooms 1–2; let the *first* truly warm, resolved theme play only when you enter the oak in Room 3, and let it bloom fully over the purring at the end.
